The expression is 4x + 5y + 3. Its terms are 4x, 5y and 3. The numbers 4 and 5 are the coefficients of x and y, and 3 is a constant — it does not move however many boxes Raju buys. The letters x and y are the variables.

The total is 200l + 160w + 50lw. Look hard at the third term: it carries both variables at once, because area depends on both. Double the length and the fencing terms grow the way you expect — but the seed cost doubles too, and doubling both sides multiplies it by four.

Why this expression is a different animal
Example 1 gave 4x + 5y + 3, where each term holds one variable. Here 50lw holds two multiplied together. Neither is a polynomial in one variable, which is what the rest of this chapter is about — but the garden is the further away of the two, and it is worth seeing why before we narrow the field.
03Degree gives the family nameSection 2.1 · constant, linear, quadratic, cubic45 XP

Once an expression uses only one variable and its powers, it is a polynomial — a univariate polynomial, to be exact. The highest power of the variable is its degree, and the degree is what the family is named after. Build one and watch it get renamed.

A term whose coefficient is zero is not there at all, so the degree is set by the highest power that survives. Setting every coefficient to zero except the constant leaves a constant polynomial: the book points out that 8 can be written 8x⁰, which is degree 0.

06The chess club's billSection 2.2 · Example 545 XP

Find the number of matches that brings the bill to exactly ₹750.

200 + 50m is a linear polynomial in m — degree 1. The joining fee is the constant, the ₹50 is the coefficient, and the coefficient is exactly the step: every extra match adds the same ₹50, never more and never less. That constant step is what makes the pattern linear.

08Work backwards to the numberExample 6 · Exercise Set 2.240 XP

Each of these becomes a linear equation once you name the unknown. Solve it and pick the answer.

The book works the first one through: let the smaller number be x, so the larger is x + 10, and x + (x + 10) = 64 gives 2x + 10 = 64, then 2x = 54 and x = 27. The two numbers are 27 and 37. Every other row is the same move — name the unknown, write the sentence as an equation, and the equation turns out to be linear.

Part 3 · Linear patterns, growth and decay
09The growing tile patternSection 2.3 · Fig. 2.455 XP

Each stage adds two tiles to the one before. Step through the stages and count — then stop counting and use the rule instead.

Tiles
1
2n − 1
—
Added since last

1, 3, 5, 7, 9 … Reach stage 15 and stage 26, and find the stage that holds 21 tiles.

At stage n the count is 2n − 1. The book gets there by noticing that the number of tiles is always one less than twice the stage number: at stage 2, 2 × 2 − 1 = 3; at stage 5, 2 × 5 − 1 = 9. Because 2n − 1 has degree 1 it is a linear polynomial, and the sequence 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 … has a constant difference of 2 — which is the coefficient of n, appearing again as the step.

10Use the rule, don't draw the tilesSection 2.3 · Think and Reflect35 XP

Once the rule is 2n − 1, questions about stage 26 stop needing a drawing.

The last two rows run the rule backwards: if 2n − 1 = 21 then 2n = 22 and n = 11, and if 2n − 1 = 47 then n = 24. A rule you can only use forwards is half a rule.

11Money going down, fares going upSection 2.3 · Examples 7 and 850 XP

Two everyday patterns, both linear, running in opposite directions. Bela starts with ₹100 and spends ₹5 a day. An auto-rickshaw charges ₹25 for the first 2 km and ₹15 for every kilometre after that.

Rule
—
Value
—
Step
—
Find the day Bela is left with ₹40, and the distance whose fare is ₹130.

Bela's money follows 100 − 5n, so on day 12 she has 100 − 60 = ₹40 and on day 20 she has nothing left. The fare is 25 + 15(n − 2) = 15n − 5 for n ≥ 2, so 10 km costs 25 + 15 × 8 = ₹145, and a ₹130 fare means 15n − 5 = 130, giving n = 9 km.

The same shape, opposite signs
Both rules are linear polynomials in n. The only difference that matters is the sign of the coefficient: −5 makes the amount fall by the same step every day, +15 makes the fare climb by the same step every kilometre. That single sign is the whole distinction between linear decay and linear growth.
12Growth or decay?Exercise Set 2.4 · Section 2.435 XP

Each situation is linear. Say which way it runs.

The test is never the size of the numbers, only the sign of the step. A phone losing ₹800 a year and a village gaining 50 people a year are the same kind of pattern pointed in opposite directions — and when you draw them in the next part, that sign becomes the direction the line tilts.

Part 4 · Linear relationships, drawn
13Two readings give you the ruleSection 2.5 · Example 1150 XP

With 10 GB costing ₹350 and 20 GB costing ₹550, the two equations are 350 = 10a + b and 550 = 20a + b. Substituting b = 350 − 10a into the second gives 550 = 20a + 350 − 10a, so 10a = 200 and a = 20, which leaves b = 150. The relationship is y = 20x + 150 — ₹20 a gigabyte on top of a fixed ₹150 a month.

14What a does: the slopeSection 2.6 · Figs. 2.9 and 2.1150 XP

Every line y = ax passes through the origin. What changes with a is how steeply it climbs — or whether it climbs at all.

Line
—
Against y = x

Direction

Make the line steeper than y = x, then gentler, then send it downhill.

When a > 1 the line is steeper than y = x; when a < 1 it is gentler; and y = x itself is equally inclined to both axes. A positive slope is linear growth drawn as a picture, a negative slope is linear decay. And the slope is not a new idea — for the tile pattern y = 2n − 1 the slope 2 is the same 2 that was the constant difference between consecutive terms.

15What b does: the y-interceptSection 2.6 · Figs. 2.13 and 2.1450 XP

Now hold the slope still and move the other number. The line does not tilt — it slides, and where it crosses the y-axis is exactly b.

Any line written y = ax + b cuts the y-axis at the point (0, b). The book's three lines cut it at A (0, 5), B (0, 3) and C (0, −2). Change b with a fixed and the lines shift but stay parallel — lines with equal slopes and different intercepts never meet. Change a and they are not parallel any more, whatever b does.
